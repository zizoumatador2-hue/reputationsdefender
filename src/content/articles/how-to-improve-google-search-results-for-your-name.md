Managing what shows up when someone Googles your name isn't about hiding anything — it's about making sure the search results are accurate, current, and actually represent you. Whether you're job hunting, running a business, or just want fewer surprises when a new client searches your name, the approach is the same: build genuine, well-structured content and give it time to rank.

## Step 1: Search Your Own Name First

Before building anything, see what's actually there. Search:

- Your full name in quotes
- Your name plus your city or employer
- Your name plus your profession
- Image search for your name

Note which results are accurate, which are outdated, and which aren't about you at all (a common issue if you share a name with someone else — in that case, see the name-disambiguation approach below).

## Step 2: Claim and Complete the Profiles That Matter

A handful of well-maintained profiles tend to outperform a scattered presence across a dozen platforms:

- **LinkedIn** — complete, current, with a real photo and detailed experience; this ranks strongly for most professional name searches
- **A personal website**, ideally on a domain matching your name, even if it's a single well-written page
- **Industry-specific platforms** relevant to your field (a portfolio site, GitHub for developers, a professional association directory)

Consistency matters: use the same name format, photo, and bio language across profiles so search engines associate them with the same identity.

## Step 3: Publish Original, Substantial Content

Thin, templated content doesn't rank well and doesn't help. What tends to work:

- A detailed "About" page that genuinely describes your background and work
- Articles, case studies, or posts that reflect real expertise
- Guest contributions to reputable, relevant publications in your field — these carry more authority than content on your own site alone

## Step 4: If You Share a Name With Someone Else

If negative results belong to a different person entirely, the fix isn't removal — it's disambiguation. Consistently including a middle name, initial, location, or profession in your own content and profiles helps search engines (and people) distinguish you from someone else with the same name.

## Step 5: Be Patient With the Timeline

New or updated content needs to be crawled, indexed, and then accumulate the signals (links, engagement, consistency over time) that affect ranking. Meaningful movement in search results for a name typically takes a few months of consistent activity — not days. There's no legitimate shortcut around that timeline, and any service claiming otherwise is overselling what's possible.

## What This Won't Do

Building positive content doesn't delete or hide anything negative that's already indexed — for that narrower question, see our guide on [how to deal with negative search results](/privacy-guides/how-to-deal-with-negative-search-results/). This strategy works alongside, not instead of, direct removal requests where those apply.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many profiles should I maintain?**
Fewer, well-maintained profiles generally outperform many abandoned ones. Two or three active, complete profiles relevant to your field is a reasonable target for most people.

**Does posting on social media help my search ranking?**
It can, particularly for major platforms with strong domain authority, but an abandoned or inactive profile often ranks poorly and can look worse than no profile at all.

**Can a personal website really compete with a news article?**
Not always, and especially not quickly — but it adds a current, accurate, controllable result to the mix, which over time changes what the overall first page looks like.
